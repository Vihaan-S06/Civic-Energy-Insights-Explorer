That's a much stronger direction. I would stop thinking of it as a "feature" and instead think of it as a platform for running energy competitions.

Instead of everyone seeing the same generic challenge, there are Organizers and Participants.

Core Concept
Energy For Everyone Challenges become events.

Someone (teacher, city, nonprofit, scout leader, company, club president, etc.) creates a challenge.

Participants join with a code.

Everyone completes missions over a set period of time.

The leaderboard updates live.

At the end, a winner is crowned.

Think of it as a mix of Kahoot + Duolingo + fantasy sports, but for clean energy.

Replayability
The biggest mistake would be making every challenge identical. Instead, each organizer should be able to customize:

Challenge duration (1 day, 1 week, 1 month)

Individual vs. team competition

Public or private access with a join code

Mission library plus custom missions

XP values and bonus multipliers

Whether evidence is required

Daily mission release schedule

Flash challenges and bonus events

Scoring rules (XP only, CO₂ saved, simulator performance, or a weighted combination)

This means a teacher, scout troop, city government, or company can all run the platform differently without changing the code.

The end result isn't just a leaderboard—it's a reusable challenge platform where organizers can create unique competitions and participants have reasons to return every day. That structure is much more scalable and engaging than a single, hardcoded community challenge.
