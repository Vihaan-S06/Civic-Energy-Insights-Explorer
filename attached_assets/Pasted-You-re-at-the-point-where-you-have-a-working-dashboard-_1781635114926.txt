You're at the point where you have a working dashboard. The biggest mistake now would be adding random features. Instead, focus on features that move the project closer to its mission:

Make energy data understandable and help people take action.

Right now, your site is very good at the "understand" part. It needs more of the "take action" part.

Priority 1: Action Hub (Highest Impact)
This was one of the strongest parts of the original project idea.

Contact Your Officials
Allow users to generate emails to:

City council members

Sustainability offices

Utility companies

Example:

"Renewable energy in my region has decreased 8% over the last year. I would like to know what plans exist to increase clean energy generation."

User enters:

Name

City

Issue

The site generates an editable email.

Personalized Recommendations
If a region has:

High fossil fuel use → suggest solar programs

High electricity prices → suggest efficiency programs

High CO₂ intensity → suggest local advocacy actions

Example:

Top Actions For Texas

1. Support community solar projects
2. Reduce peak-hour electricity use
3. Contact local utility about renewable targets
This immediately makes the data useful.

Community Project Ideas
Users can submit ideas:

Install solar at schools
Create EV charging stations
Community battery storage
Tree planting initiative
Display them publicly.

If I Were Building This For a Competition
My next three features would be:

1. Interactive U.S. Map
2. Action Hub with email templates
3. Explain This Number panels
Those three features would transform the project from:

"A dashboard that displays energy data"

into

"A civic platform that helps citizens understand and influence energy policy"

which is much closer to the original LocalEnergy Lens vision and would stand out much more to judges, teachers, and community organizations.
